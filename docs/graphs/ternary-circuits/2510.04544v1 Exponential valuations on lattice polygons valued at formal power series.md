# Exponential valuations on lattice polygons valued at formal power series
Böröczky, Domokos, Freyer, Haberl & Li, arXiv:2510.04544v1 [math.MG] (2025). Classifies valuations on lattice polygons ℤ² valued in the formal power series ring Q[[x,y]] that are equivariant under the affine unimodular group G(ℤ²) = ℤ² ⋊ GL(2,ℤ). The continuum analogue (Li–Ma) is unique up to scale (only the Laplace transform), but on the *lattice* there are "many more" — the classification is rigid but parametrized by a D4-invariant series ϱ satisfying a functional equation. Sits in the same lineage as our other valuation papers (2411.09383, 1903.06044) and is the sibling paper by the same group.

## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **C1 — "On the lattice, equivariant valuations are unique (like the continuum)" is FALSE.** Li–Ma (Thm 1): in the continuum, continuous equivariant valuations valued at functions are `c·L` — *one* object up to scale. The paper's headline: "as it turns out there are many more" — on lattice polygons valued at formal power series there is a whole family (parametrized by `g` and ϱ). **Discreteness breaks the uniqueness.** (counter-to: the continuum uniqueness; same moral as our counting-measure thesis.)
- **C2 — "The exponential is the point" is a trap.** The exp factors in (3) are a *formal* character of the translation group; after the ♯/† transform the exponentials cancel out and the content is a plain algebraic functional equation (8) with integer-ish structure (Bernoulli numbers). The analytic-looking object is combinatorial underneath. (reversal: exponential-looking ≠ analytic.)
- **C3 — "Simple = trivial" is FALSE.** Simple valuations (vanishing on segments/points) are not the degenerate case: they are the parametrized core (ϱ ∈ V ↔ `Z(T)` via (9)), and the non-simple part is a small correction `Z1` built from f0/f1. The low-dimensional data determine everything (Prop 6(ii): point + segment + triangle ⇒ the whole valuation).

## 5. One-liner

**Discreteness breaks uniqueness: equivariant valuations on lattice polygons are *many*, not one (unlike the continuum Laplace case), and are rigidly classified by a point, a segment, and a D4-invariant power series satisfying one functional equation — the same moral as our counting-measure thesis (the integer lattice adds structure the continuum lacks) and an ANALOGY template for how "symmetry + additivity ⇒ classification" could frame our Z₆ norm, but it contributes nothing to the RTL layer.**
