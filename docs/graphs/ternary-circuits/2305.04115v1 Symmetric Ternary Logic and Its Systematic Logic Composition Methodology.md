## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **"Symmetric" here is CYCLIC, ours is REFLECTION.** ROTATE is a 3-cycle 0→2→1→0 (Z₃ action on the digit set). Our balanced ternary's symmetry is negation (+1↔−1, 0 fixed) — a reflection, the free digit-swap. Same word "symmetric ternary", different group. (Our Z₆ contains their Z₃: ROTATE = 120° rotation = multiply by ω² — see §4.)
- **Boundedness costs expressiveness.** "0 in x⊼0 cannot be eliminated" — the third operator's constant cannot be simplified away (§IV). Counter to the naive "ternary is strictly richer for free".
- **Distributivity is only one-sided**: x+(y⊼z) = (x+y)⊼(x+z) holds, but the reversed order x⊼(y×z) fails — the "lattice" is not a distributive lattice in the usual sense.
- **Digits are {0,1,2}, not balanced.** The whole paper is positive (unbalanced) ternary with a cyclic symmetry; it never uses {−1,0,+1} — our digit set and our carry rule are absent.

## 5. One-liner

"Any ternary function is built from ONE 3-cycle + THREE min/max-style dyadic operators" — a functional-completeness theorem for a small ternary gate set, structurally parallel to our {tneg,tand,tor,tmul,tadd1} cell set, but on unbalanced digits {0,1,2} with a cyclic (not reflection) symmetry, so it confirms the *shape* of our approach, not the cells themselves.
