## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **Symmetry is a knife that cuts both ways: INVARIANCE kills, COVARIANCE preserves.** SL(2,ℤ)-invariant functions are forced constant (ergodicity); but GL(2,ℤ)-*covariant* valuations are a rich continuum. "More symmetry ⇒ more structure" is false here — invariance is the degenerate limit. This is a caution for any of our claims that lean on invariance alone.
- **τ collision:** this paper's τ is the GOLDEN RATIO (1+√5)/2; our τ is 2π (full-turn, Packing.lean). Same symbol, different constant — come-to-terms before quoting.
- **The paper is analytic (real/complex measurable functions), NOT integer counting.** Its objects are exponential/Laplace-weighted — a generalization away from the counting measure. Only the Ehrhart fragment (N10) is genuinely a counting object. Ian's "counts not probabilities" maps to Ehrhart, not to the exponential machinery.
- **The valuation axiom is inclusion-exclusion, not additivity-on-disjoint-union** — it needs P∪Q convex, so it is a lattice-measure, not a σ-algebra measure: a structural caution for mapping "measure theory" onto our lattice.

## 5. One-liner

"A rigidity-vs-richness theorem: GL(2,ℤ)-covariant valuations on lattice polygons are a rich continuum parametrized by functions on golden-ratio-slit fundamental domains, while full invariance collapses to constants — giving us (a) the general frame that contains our Z₆ isotropy as the finite subgroup, (b) Ehrhart's integer counting as the DIRECT anchor for 'counts not probabilities', and (c) a τ-symbol collision to avoid."
