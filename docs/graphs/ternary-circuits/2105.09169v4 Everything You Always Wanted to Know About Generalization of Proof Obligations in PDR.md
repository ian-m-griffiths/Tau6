## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **Ternary logic's imprecision (reversal to naive trust):** X∧¬X = X in 01X-logic, but the *real* gate is constant-0 — so 01X-simulation can *fail to remove* a bit that IS removable, and worse, its outputs are not guaranteed POs (only lifting/IGBG confirm). The three-valued abstraction is *lossy*, not exact. (§IV-A1, §VI-A)
- **Lifting can be silently WRONG, not just weak:** for non-left-total T (invariant constraints), lifting produces spurious counterexamples — the paper had to *deactivate* lifting in ic3ref to get correct results (qspiflash benchmarks, §VII-A3). Generalization method correctness depends on transition-relation properties (left-total, right-unique) — a property-*table* is a core deliverable (§I, §IV-B).
- **Free ≠ better:** "free" variants (choice of m not fixed) mostly *underperform* their "fix" counterparts in practice (§VI-D, VII-A1a) — counter to the intuition that more freedom = more generalization.
- **Exact ≈ cheaper than thought:** greedy QBF reaches 99.8% of MaxQBF's (optimal) quality — the "expensive exact methods" line is softer than it looks (§VII-A1a, Table I).

## 5. One-liner

**This paper gives us the exact recipe and the exact warnings for formal-verifying our ternary RTL:** its 01X two-rail encoding is *literally* our 2-bit trit code (00=null/X, 01=+1, 10=−1, 11=never/forbidden), so MS01X-style SAT/MaxSAT lifting and PDR/IC3 apply directly — with the invariant-constraint trap (non-left-total T breaks lifting) flagged as the first thing to design around; our 11=never state doubles as both the don't-care for a MaxSAT verifier and the AIGER-style dead-end repair state.
