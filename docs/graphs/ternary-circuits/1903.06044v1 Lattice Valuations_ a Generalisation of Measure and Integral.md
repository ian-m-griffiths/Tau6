## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **Measure and integral are the SAME kind of object** — the paper's thesis is that the Lebesgue measure and Lebesgue integral, usually built separately, are both lattice valuations; the "distinction" is a convention, not a mathematical split. Counter-to the standard treatment.
- **Modularity is the primitive; additivity is derived** — for additive set functions, additivity + positivity ⇒ valuation, but valuations need NOT be additive (∨/∧ modularity is weaker). The paper works with the weaker primitive.
- **R-completeness is the right hypothesis, not Dedekind completeness** — Q fails R-completeness, the lexicographic plane passes; and σ-Dedekind completeness is *essential* (Remark 216: a group in which *every* sequence has a supremum is {0} — the non-triviality of the notion reverses intuition).
- **"Completion" here ≠ "completion" in the project's memory pipeline** — mathematical completion (closing under Π/Σ limits) vs our `consolidate`/down opcode "completion" (merging temp .latx). Terminological collision, not an identity.
- **The hierarchy genuinely needs uncountably many steps** (Borel Hierarchy Theorem): no finite (or countable) alternation of Π/Σ closes the construction — against the intuition that a "completion" is a small step.

## 5. One-liner: what this paper gives us

A fully worked theory that measure and integral are both lattice valuations (monotone + modular into an ordered Abelian group) with distances, completions and convergence theorems — legitimizing our per-trit energy as a counting valuation and our counts-not-probabilities stance, and supplying the min+max=sum identity our ternary adder already relies on.
