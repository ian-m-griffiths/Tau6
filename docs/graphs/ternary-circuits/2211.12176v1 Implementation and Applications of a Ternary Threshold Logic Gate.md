## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **Threshold ≠ free:** the TLG is *clocked and level-sensitive* — every TLG-based gate carries a flip-flop, which is why AND/OR *lose* to standard CMOS (they needed extra ternary D-latches for synchronization, §7.1). Reversal of "threshold logic is a pure win."
- **Ternary density vs manufacturability trade:** the paper's key reversal of the CNTFET narrative — CNTFETs make ternary *easy* (threshold via chirality) but are *unscalable* (~15K transistors max, §2.2); multi-Vt CMOS makes ternary *harder* (must add low-Vt devices) but is *scalable*. Our project's "silicon is binary-ecosystem-locked" caution (TERNARY_PROCESSOR.md §1.5) is directly echoed.
- **The output of the TLG is binary, not ternary:** o ∈ {0,2} — the "ternary gate" is a ternary→binary comparator plus transmission-gate routing of Vdd/Vbb/ground. The actual 3-valued logic happens in the *muxing*, not the gate core. Reversal of "the gate computes in ternary."
- **sgn is a magnitude decision, not arithmetic:** o = sgn(Σwᵢxᵢ + ε) + 1 classifies by weighted sum — this is threshold/neuron semantics, not the digit-sum *carry* semantics our tadd1 implements (though both are "sum then threshold").

## 5. One-liner

**This paper independently re-derives our tadd1's core idea — a ternary gate that thresholds a weighted digit sum (sgn(Σwᵢxᵢ+ε)+1 = our s≥2/s≤−2 carry rule) — and shows it is silicon-realizable in multi-Vt CMOS, while its ternary D-latch/flip-flop (Appendix A.3) is the missing sequential cell our RTL (binary FFs only) should steal; its honest failure (AND/OR lose to CMOS) is the caution that not every ternary gate family wins on delay/area.**
