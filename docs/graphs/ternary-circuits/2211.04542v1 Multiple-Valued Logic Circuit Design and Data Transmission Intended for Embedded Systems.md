## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **"Ternary saves energy" is conditional on *how* you make the middle level:** creating logic-1 (Vdd/2) with diode-connected divider transistors *increases static power* (a direct Vdd→ground path). The thesis's own headline savings come only from switching to an *external* Vdd/2 rail. Reversal: naive 3-level design is an energy *liability*.
- **Unbalanced ≠ balanced:** the whole thesis runs on (0, Vdd/2, Vdd) voltage levels; the balanced (−1,0,1) form is mentioned (§1.3) and then abandoned. The asymmetry (three levels, no sign symmetry) is a different animal from our balanced push/pull/null.
- **CNFET, not CMOS-silicon:** the energy numbers are HSPICE simulations of carbon-nanotube FETs, not a fabricated chip; scalability caveat (echoes TERNARY_PROCESSOR.md §1.5: "no large-scale ternary logic in practice").
- **Their forbidden (1,1) code is *exactly* our 11=NEVER:** the thesis's "This Case Does Not Exist" (Table 5.4) is the same engineering fact we prove in Lean (`encode_never_both`) — a nice independent confirmation.
- **Transistor-count is a proxy, not the metric:** the "≥73% fewer transistors" headline does not translate 1:1 to energy (their own power model N15 is linear in N but also in Vdd²·f·CL).

## 5. One-liner

**This thesis is our strongest DIRECT engineering counterpart: it builds the exact same objects we have in RTL — STI/negation, min/max gates, a balanced-vs-unbalanced ternary adder family, and a binary↔ternary converter whose two-rail-one-forbidden code (0→00, 1→02, 2→20, 11 "does not exist") is literally our 2-bit trit encoding, with `encode_never_both` confirmed in silicon-simulated hardware — and its energy claims (88–99% PDP cuts) are the measured-evidence playbook our Yosys power pass should copy, minus the CNFET level-coding we don't share.**
