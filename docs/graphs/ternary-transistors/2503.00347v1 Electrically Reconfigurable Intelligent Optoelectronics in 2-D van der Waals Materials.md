## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **VERDICT: this is NOT a reconfigurable-polarity FET and NOT a native 3-state device — it is a review of *analog*, gate-tunable optoelectronics.** The premise "reconfigurable polarity (n↔p↔ambipolar)" is a mis-characterization of this paper: "reconfigurability" here means continuous electrical tuning of *optical* response (refractive index, birefringence, photo-responsivity, bandgap, Berry curvature), not a discrete off/n/p logic state. The hunt target is absent on arrival; the question collapses to whether the nearest analogue is tunable-binary or native-ternary, and it is tunable-binary/analog.
- **The ternary-shaped objects are signed weights, not trits.** The only place a sign (−/+) genuinely appears is photocurrent/responsivity (Mennel's ±R MVM, MoTe2's −800→+800 mA/W, MoS2 MSM's ± states). That maps onto push/pull *as an analogy* — and it is deployed as a *signed analog weight for matrix-vector multiplication*, not as a per-trit logic level. A signed weight ≠ a trit.
- **The "null" is not free — it is a fragile bias crossing.** Zero photocurrent exists only at a finely-tuned gate-bias point between positive and negative regimes, and drifts; it is not a robust, zero-energy, resting state. The Tau thesis's "null trit costs ~0" has no support here; the null is the *hardest* point to hold, not the cheapest.
- **"5 states (5 digital bits)" is the flattery trap, stated explicitly.** 5 non-volatile conductance levels ≈ 2.32 bits, not 5 bits. The paper's own multi-level memory devices (5 / 11 / 51 states) are *analog* levels quantized by write-pulse precision — the exact class of device the lens warns against ("native 3-state devices are usually 0.63× native floor flattery"). Here it is worse: they are multi-level *analog* memories, not even 3-state.
- **Non-volatility is storage, not compute, and it costs.** Ferroelectric domains, charge traps, and vacancy migration buy state *retention* (weights) at the price of write pulses and limited level count — the opposite of a free, fast, low-energy compute state. Retention cost is exactly what Tau must avoid in transport; this paper pays it to store weights.
- **Reconfigurability = analog multiplexing, not radix-3 addressing.** The "one device does many jobs" win (C2) is achieved by *sweeping* a continuous knob (gate/displacement field) and reconstructing with an algorithm (M4/M5), not by holding 3 discrete states. The argument *shape* matches "3ⁿ addressing wins," but the mechanism is time-multiplexed analog, not a larger state radix.
- **The paper never mentions ternary, balanced ternary, or trits anywhere.** The word "three" appears only in mundane counts (three classes, three regions, three telecom bands). There is no compute-architecture claim to flatter or refute on the addressing/transport/compute trilemma.
- **Net:** orthogonal to the hunt. It offers no native transistor whose intrinsic physics yields off/n/p; its sign-carrying photocurrent is a continuous signed weight (tunable binary/analog), its null is a bias point (not free), and its headline "5 digital bits" from 5 states is the flattery trap verbatim.

## 5. One-liner

A review of *analog*, gate-reconfigurable 2-D optoelectronics whose "reconfigurability" is continuous electrical tuning of optical response (index/birefringence/responsivity/bandgap/Berry curvature) — not a reconfigurable-polarity FET and not a native 3-state device; its only ternary-shaped objects are signed photocurrent (±/0) and 4-way junction polarity (nn/np/pn/pp), deployed as continuous signed weights for matrix-vector multiplication with a fragile bias-point "null," so it is a tunable binary/analog platform that, in its own "5 states = 5 digital bits" line, lands in the 0.63×-flattery trap rather than the native-trit hunt.
