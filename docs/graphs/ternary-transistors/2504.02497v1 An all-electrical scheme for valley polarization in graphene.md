## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **VERDICT (lead): this is NOT a native ternary or polarity-encoded trit device — it is a clean BINARY (K/K′) transport axis, and its "polarity" is only an analogy to trit sign.** The paper never claims a 3-state device; it claims a *polarization efficiency* over exactly two valleys. There is no native push/pull/null floor here to be flattered — there is a signed binary difference.
- **Valley is a second (third) BINARY axis, not a ternary state.** The paper's own words are "two inequivalent valleys, labeled K and K′." Adding valley to charge and spin gives a *third independent degree of freedom* — a 2×2×2 = 8-state space (3 bits), i.e. 2ⁿ addressing, NOT 3ⁿ. The headline phrase "adding a third dimension to conventional electronics and spintronics" means "third DoF," not "third value of a trit." This is the single most important reversal: *a third axis ≠ a ternary digit.*
- **The hexagon does not deliver ternary here — it delivers binary.** Graphene's honeycomb lattice shares the 6-fold symmetry of ℤ[ω] (ω = e^{iπ/3}), but its hexagonal Brillouin zone splits into TWO inequivalent corners (K, K′) — a binary valley index — and the two-atom basis adds a further binary sublattice (A/B) pseudospin. The same hexagonal geometry Tau exploits for 3 directions yields 2×2 binary axes here. (Geometry mapping: ANALOGY; the "hexagon ⇒ ternary" instinct is OURS and it fails.)
- **The null is the default, and the ± states are the expensive ones — the exact inverse of Tau's "free null."** η = 0 (balanced K/K′ occupation) is the unbiased equilibrium, but a *symmetric* current still flows — valley null is NOT a no-transport state. The polarized ± states are what cost: they require engineered transverse-momentum matching (ky,F ≈ half the K–K′ separation). Tau wants a ~free null and cheap ±; this device gives a cheap-looking null that still carries charge, and expensive ±.
- **Signal and carrier fight each other (the native-floor trap, in reverse).** Disorder *raises* conductance (enhanced DOS) while *lowering* η (intervalley scattering). Length raises reflection count, lowering η. In every knob the paper turns, the thing that carries the bit (charge conductance) and the thing that encodes it (valley imbalance) move in opposite directions. A "3-state" reading would be exactly the 0.63× flattery: you cannot get a useful tri-valued output when the polarity vanishes as soon as the device conducts.
- **η = +1 at the Dirac point, with zero conductance — polarization without transport is useless.** η "shoots up" as μg → 0 precisely where Gg → 0. A state that is perfectly "polarized" but carries no current is not a transistor; it is a disconnected flag.
- **η is continuous, not discrete.** η ∈ [−1,+1] is a signed real (a polarization *efficiency*), so "push / null / pull" would have to be imposed as thresholds on a continuum — the hallmark of an analog quantity being dressed up as a trit, not a native 3-state.

## 5. One-liner

Graphene valley polarization is a signed but fundamentally **binary** (K/K′) transport axis whose "polarity" only analogizes to trit sign — the null is the balanced default that still carries charge, the ± states are the expensive engineered ones, and every tuning knob trades the carrier against the signal — so valleytronics adds a third *bit*, not a native ternary *trit*.
