## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **C1 — Gauge-priority reversal (N19↔N20/N21).** The book treats probability as the goal: the uniform measure on ℝ and ℕ has mass ∞ "so is not a probability measure. Damn" (L1049–53, L1105–06), and P=0 is posed as the deep problem that forces measure theory (Fact 1.29, L1088–89). Our system inverts: integer counts are the primitive ("counts, not probabilities", STATE_NOTE), and the probability measure is the same measure rescaled to mass 1 — a benign register shift by T (GAUGE_VARIANTS §1B). Same arithmetic, opposite gauge priority. The book itself supplies the constructive evidence for our side: Prop 6.19 builds Lebesgue integration from renormalized counting on the dyadic lattice (L6214–28). Calibration: DIRECT (the math), OURS (the priority). CLAIMED in the book as motivation, not proved as a theorem.
- **C2 — "lattice" name collision (N14↔N22).** The paper's "lattice" (L3185, L13960–67) is the *partition poset* P(k) of set partitions, on which Möbius inversion lives — a combinatorics object, no geometric lattice. Our "lattice" is the hexagonal ℤ[ω] lattice. Come-to-terms; no math relation. counter-to (terminology only).
- **C3 — "orthogonal" name collision (N18).** Radon–Nikodym's λ ⊥ µ = *mutually singular* measures (Def 7.32(2), L7352), and "discrete measures are orthogonal to Lebesgue" (L7361–68) — a genuinely different use of "orthogonal" than our ⊥ axis (correlation/surprise, GAUGE_VARIANTS §1D). counter-to (terminology only).
- **C4 — Haar vs isotropy scope.** The book's Haar invariance is under ALL translations of the group (µ(E)=µ(gE)=µ(Eg), L13426–29); our G1 is invariance under the 6 rotations only. Same shape, different group — ANALOGY, not DIRECT (do not upgrade).

## 5. One-liner

**"Measure theory is the formal home of counts vs probabilities: the counting measure is the canonical integer-valued measure, the probability measure is the same measure renormalized to mass 1, finite-group Haar IS the normalized counting measure, and even continuous Lebesgue/Haar integration is constructed from counting on lattices — with Haar integrals reducing to integer partition combinatorics — which is direct, in-paper evidence for Ian's thesis that measure theory is how we got to Eisenstein integers and 'gauge becomes a natural and cheap INTEGER thing'."**
