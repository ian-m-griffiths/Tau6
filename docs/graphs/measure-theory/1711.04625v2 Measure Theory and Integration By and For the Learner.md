## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **C1 — "measure theory is about continuous sets" is FALSE**: the counting measure (04.14b) is the tutorial's own counterweight — it is the *conceptually* most important measure ("very important one in this theory from a conceptual point of view"), and all other nontrivial measures are "either constructed or derived from constructed measures" (NOTA-BENE) — discrete counting is the base, not the exception.
- **C2 — "the integral is built from the measure" is only half the story**: the Daniell route (Ch 10) builds the *integral first* and derives the measure from it — the same inversion as the Riesz functional in paper 1409.2662. The measure↔integral arrow runs both ways.
- **C3 — measurability is not the same in the counting world**: exercises (Doc 05-03) push the learner to re-derive classical facts for the counting measure, where "null sets" are empty sets and convergence is plain summation — the translation is not automatic.

## 5. One-liner

**The counting measure is *defined* here as the sum of Dirac masses with `ν(A) = Card(A)` — the exact object behind Ian's "counts not probabilities" — and the paper's twin lessons (integration against it is a plain sum; every other measure is constructed from it) are the textbook statement of "gauge becomes a natural and cheap INTEGER thing"; the translation-invariant Lebesgue measure is the Haar/isotropy analog on the ℤ[ω] side (still ANALOGY, no hex Haar theorem yet).**
