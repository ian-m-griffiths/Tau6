# Quantum Measure Theory: A New Interpretation
Yousef Ghazi-Tabatabai, PhD thesis, Imperial College (arXiv:0906.0294v1, 2009). A histories-based reformulation of quantum mechanics as a *quantum measure* µ; the naive "one history is real" interpretation fails on the Peres-Kochen-Specker system; Sorkin's **co-events** (truth-valuation maps) replace histories as the ontology; the **multiplicative scheme** wins the comparison of co-event schemes.

## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **C1 — "A quantum system needs a probability measure" is FALSE.** µ is *not* a probability measure: it violates Kolmogorov additivity (only `I₃=0`), can exceed 1 (§8.1), and unitality is optional (N16). Probabilities exist only on decoherent partitions. The primary object is a measure that refuses sum-to-1 normalization.
- **C2 — "One history is real" (naive realism) FAILS.** PKS: every history is precluded — no single element of the sample space can be real. Ontology must move to *sets/maps* (co-events), i.e. reality is not a point but a coarse-grained region.
- **C3 — "More logic = better" is FALSE.** Classical (Boolean-homomorphism) co-events are the *failure* case; the winner relaxes logic (multiplicativity only) — and even then, the theory cannot fully re-express dynamics in terms of its own potential realities (Thm 6).
- **C4 — Decoherence is too weak / too strong for "classical".** The consistent-histories partition problem: two decoherent coarse grainings need not share a decoherent fine graining (double-slit, §1.3); conversely the multiplicative scheme needs *preclusive separability*, not decoherence, for classicality (§7.6).

## 5. One-liner

**The quantum measure is a counting-measure-style object that refuses probability normalization (sum-to-1 is optional, additivity fails, values exceed 1) — direct philosophical support for our "counts not probabilities" gauge — plus a structural analogy for our sym/skew split (Hermitian real/imaginary = correlation/wedge) and a caution that ontology must live in coarse-grained regions (filters/bands), not single atoms.**
