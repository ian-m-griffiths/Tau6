# Topics of Measure Theory on Infinite Dimensional Spaces
José Velhinho, arXiv:2312.04365v1 [math.FA] (2023). A short review of measures on infinite-dimensional spaces from the **projective** perspective: product measures, projective limits, Gaussian measures, and transformation properties (quasi-invariance, ergodicity) — with the algebraic dual E^a as the "universal home" for measures and Minlos-type support theorems. Written from a QFT/mathematical-physics background. No abstract Wiener spaces; no Riesz representation; **no lattice measures or lattice valuations in this paper** (those live in the companion lattice-fluids review, 0205648).

## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **R1 — "Infinite products of measures exist" holds ONLY for normalized measures.** The unique-product theorem (Thm 4, L323) requires each factor to be a probability measure; the natural "uniform" product ∏dxk **does not exist** (L360–364). Reversal: in infinite dimensions the sum-to-1 normalization is not cosmetic — it is the *necessary condition* for a product measure to exist. This is the paper's sharpest counts-vs-probabilities statement.
- **R2 — "Every space has a translation-invariant measure" (Haar intuition) FAILS in infinite dimensions.** Haar gives uniqueness on locally compact groups (L1178–1182), but there is **no non-trivial translation-invariant σ-finite Borel measure** on an infinite-dimensional separable Banach space (ball-packing argument, L1183–1194). Reversal: invariance and finite-ness are incompatible at infinite dimension; you must give up one.
- **R3 — "The measure lives where you define it" is FALSE: the measure lives on a proper subspace.** Minlos/Bochner–Minlos (Thm 9–10, L822–877): smoothness of the characteristic function forces the support onto E′ ⊂ E^a — "the space where the measure is actually supported is at the end determined by the inner product itself" (L888–890). A constant χ collapses the measure to a **Dirac at the null element** (L725).
- **R4 — "Invariant ⇒ probability (sum 1)" is FALSE; invariance and normalization are independent gauges.** The translation-invariant measure on R is the Lebesgue measure — infinite, never normalized (L1178); only on compact groups (tori, the Bohr compactification) does invariance + finiteness give a probability measure (L596–608). Count vs prob is a free gauge choice, exactly the paper's own framing "normalized if and only if χ(0) = 1" (L683, L693).

## 5. One-liner

**This review supplies the measure-theoretic *necessity* behind "counts, not probabilities": in infinite dimensions a normalized (sum-to-1) measure exists only by fiat or as a Gaussian, while the translation-invariant object that always exists is the integer counting measure — and our independence null `E = f(a)f(b)/T` is literally the product measure, so the [count]↔[prob] gauge is the same register shift as counting-Haar ↔ normalized-Haar, making the gauge a natural, cheap, integer thing.**
