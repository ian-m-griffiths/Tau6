# Measures and all that — A Tutorial
E.-E. Doberkat, arXiv:1409.2662v3 (2014). A measure-theory tutorial aimed at Markov transition systems: σ-algebras and Borel/analytic spaces, measurable selections, integration (Daniell integral, Riesz representation), product measures (Fubini, projective limits), the weak topology on spaces of measures, and Lp-spaces with Radon–Nikodym and disintegration — with case studies in stochastic Kripke models and bisimulation.

## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **C1 — "in the continuum you must give up point-to-point probabilities"** (§1). The overview's central reversal vs discrete systems: in a large/continuous space, transition *probabilities between points* are no longer available and must be replaced by *transition measures to sets*. Ian's world is the discrete one — the tutorial itself concedes "discrete measures are sometimes difficult enough to handle" (§1). The continuum is the harder gauge, not the more fundamental one.
- **C2 — the σ-algebra inversion** (§2.1.1): "instead of observing a measure assigning a real number to a set, we take a set and have it act on measures." Evaluation is dualized: sets become observables *on* measures. This is the same flip as our register ladder — the same invariant read from the object or from its dual.

## 4. Map-to-current-system (lens) — MEASURE THEORY

|---------------|-----------|-------------|----------|

| **probability measure** `µ(X)=1` vs arbitrary σ-finite measure (N3) | `[prob]` gauge `p(a)p(b) = E/T` vs `[count]` gauge `E = f(a)f(b)/T` — a register shift by `T` | **DIRECT** | GAUGE_VARIANTS §1B: "a measuring-stick gauge … a register shift by T" — normalizing to total mass 1 is exactly the prob register; ranking-invariant, benign |

## 5. One-liner

**Confirms the counting measure is the foundation, not a special case: discrete Dirac combinations are *dense* in all of measure theory (Prop 2.194), the product-measure null is our E = f(a)f(b)/T, and "probability = measure normalized to 1" is exactly our count→prob register shift — direct textbook support for "gauge becomes a cheap integer thing".**
