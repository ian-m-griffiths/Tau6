# Explicitly stable Fundamental Measure Theory models for classical density functional theory
J. F. Lutsko, arXiv:2009.09390v1 (2020). Re-examines tensorial Fundamental Measure Theory (FMT) — a classical density functional theory for hard spheres — under the recent global-stability requirement (the excess functional must be bounded below). Shows explicit stability is achievable only at the cost of low-density accuracy, constructs the "explicitly stable" esFMT(A,B) family (A,B ≥ 0), and benchmarks it against White Bear across fluids, walls, and solids.

> **Terminology warning (come-to-terms):** "measure" in FMT means *weighted density* — a convolution kernel integral of the density profile (η, s, v, T) — NOT a measure in the Lebesgue sense. The folder lens applies, but every mapping below must be read with this collision resolved: FMT-measures are local averages, not σ-additive set functions.

## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **C1 — "stability and accuracy are compatible" is FALSE**: the paper's central reversal — within the FMT paradigm, *explicit* global stability (bounded below) forces giving up low-density accuracy (and the PY direct correlation function); you cannot have both. (§II.B, §III.A)
- **C2 — the most sophisticated existing models are the unstable ones**: Tarazona's tensor functional (A = −B = 3/2) — the state of the art — violates the positivity condition (A,B ≥ 0), "most likely the source of their instability" (§II.C).
- **C3 — dimensional crossover has "lost cases"**: configurations of overlapping cavities (Fig 1c,d) *cannot* be captured by the FMT ansatz at all, so the crossover method has a hard representational ceiling (§II.C).

## 5. One-liner

**A physics-side confirmation that "hard-core exclusion = at most one per cell" is a real, deep constraint (0D cavities, dimensional crossover) — structurally the same exclusion as our `11=NEVER` trit — and a lesson in gauge discipline: the paper trades accuracy for guaranteed stability, exactly as we trade classical theorems for a consistent integer gauge; but its "measures" are weighted densities, not counting measures, so the direct measure-theory payoff is elsewhere.**
