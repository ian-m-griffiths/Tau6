# The Classical Theorems of Measure Theory in connection with the Statistical convergence and some remarks on Steinhaus' Theorem
C. Papachristodoulos, arXiv:1702.07154v1 (2017). Studies **statistical convergence** (Zygmund/Steinhaus/Fast) of sequences of measurable functions on `([0,1), Σ, λ)`: improves Steinhaus' theorem via a measurability (density-existence) condition, and asks which classical measure-theory theorems survive when "converges" is replaced by "converges statistically" — almost-uniformly (yes), Cauchy-in-measure/Riesz (yes), Egorov (no), dominated convergence (yes).

## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **C1 — Egorov's theorem does not survive the statistical gauge** (Ex 3.6). The paper's headline reversal: the classical chain "a.e. ⇒ almost-uniformly" (Egorov) breaks for statistical convergence — a density-1 subsequence argument shows st-a.e. ≠ st-a.u. The classical theorems that survive are exactly the "measure-side" ones (Riesz/Cauchy-in-measure, dominated convergence); the "pointwise-side" ones do not.
- **C2 — convergence in measure is *stronger* than statistical convergence in measure, yet does NOT imply st-a.e.** (Ex 2.4): a sequence can converge in measure classically while failing st-a.e. — the standard implication inverts direction when read statistically.
- **C3 — measurability is not monotone**: `(a_n)` measurable does not follow from `(|a_n|)` measurable (Rem 2.2(ii)) — the absolute value can restore density existence. Non-obvious, and a warning against assuming the counting-like density structure is well-behaved.
