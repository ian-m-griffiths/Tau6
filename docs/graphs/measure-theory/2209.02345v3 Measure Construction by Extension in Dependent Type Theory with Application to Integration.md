## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **Conventions are chosen for the algebra, not for the math** (§2.1): ∞−∞ := −∞ (vs Boldo's 0) is a gauge decision that buys associativity; the "same" theorem set lives on both conventions. Direct parallel to our E₆₀⇄E₁₂₀ / τ-π convention bridges.
- **Lists are the wrong encoding for simple functions** (§6.2, §7): the naive sorted-list representation makes proofs 5× longer (118 vs 23 lines); hierarchy-of-structures beats concrete encodings. Reversal of the "most concrete encoding is easiest" intuition.
- **σ-algebra-as-smallest vs σ-algebra-as-structure** (§3.3, §7): defining generated σ-algebra via an inductive predicate (Boldo) blocks inheritance across the class hierarchy; the paper's `smallest` predicate keeps genericity. A formalization-strategy reversal.
- **mathlib does measures differently** (§7): Lean's mathlib skips the semiring hierarchy entirely — measures ARE outer measures + Carathéodory. Same theorems, different architecture; the paper is honest that neither route was previously done for semirings.

## 4. Map-to-current-system (lens)

**FLAG — DIRECT relevance to formalizing measures in Lean.** This paper is the blueprint for measure-in-dependent-type-theory: it proves the semiring→Carathéodory extension route is formalizable (Coq), and documents that Lean's mathlib already has the outer-measure/Carathéodory route, Fubini, Radon–Nikodým, and Haar measure in Lean (van Doorn, ITP 2021). For our project: our `Residual.lean`/`Registers.lean` facts (E = f(a)f(b)/T, `sum_E_row`, `sum_residual_eq_zero`, δ/O/E ladder) are finite-counting-measure statements; mathlib's `MeasureTheory` on finite types is the natural host for upgrading them from bespoke arithmetic to real measure-theoretic claims, and this paper shows the machinery needed if we ever want the extension-theorem structure in Lean (which on finite sets is trivial — every set measurable, no outer-measure covers needed).

## 5. One-liner

**DIRECT relevance to Lean**: this paper gives us the two proven routes for formalizing measures in dependent type theory (semiring-extension in Coq; outer-measure/Carathéodory + Haar in Lean's mathlib), and shows our counting-measure facts are the trivial finite special case where all the machinery collapses — so formalizing "gauge as a cheap integer thing" in Lean needs none of the extension apparatus this paper had to build.
