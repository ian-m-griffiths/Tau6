# Mathematical Foundations of Probability Theory
G. S. Lo, SPAS Books (2018), arXiv:1808.01713v1. Probability theory founded on measure theory (companion to arXiv:1711.04625): the measure-theoretic re-reading of terminology, random variables and laws, **independence defined as the joint law being the product measure of the marginals**, cumulative distribution functions as the dual of Lebesgue–Stieltjes measures, expectation via the counting measure, Gauss measures, convergence of random variables, inequalities, conditional expectation via Radon–Nikodym, and classical asymptotic theorems.

## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **C1 — "probability is the fundamental object" is FALSE here**: the book's whole premise is the reversal — probability theory is a *sub-branch* of measure theory; laws are image measures, expectations are integrals, conditional expectation is a Radon–Nikodym derivative. The measure is primary, probability is the normed special case. (Matches our gauge reading: probability = count register, not a separate theory.)
- **C2 — independence is NOT "no correlation"**: it is defined structurally as *product measure* (Def 3), with the expectation-factorization (3.3) as its equivalent form — correlation is a derived, weaker notion. Our residual `r = O−E` measures deviation from exactly this product-measure null, not from correlation.
- **C3 — the counting measure is used where the Lebesgue one is expected**: expectation formulas (DF1/DF2) are derived *with respect to the counting measure* — the discrete sum is not an approximation of the integral, it is the integral in a different gauge.
