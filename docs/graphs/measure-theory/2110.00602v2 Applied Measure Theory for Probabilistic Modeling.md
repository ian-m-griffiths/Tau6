## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **"Discrete vs continuous" is a false dichotomy** (§1): distribution libraries type-split discrete/continuous; measures unify them (point/line/plane/space measures, spike-and-slab). Counter-to the standard library design.
- **Normalization is the enemy of computation** (§10): 76% of LKJ runtime is spent computing a normalization constant MCMC never needs — push it into the base measure and defer it. Counter-to "normalize first, compute later".
- **Density is NOT intrinsic** (§4): logdensity is only defined relative to a base measure; "no natural log-density for a given measure". Counter-to the common intuition that a distribution has THE density.
- **Locality replaces globality** (§3): you can't afford global absolute-continuity checks; work with local domination. Reversal of the global flavor of RN theory.

**Lens verdict**: this is the strongest direct support in the folder for "counts not probabilities" — it makes the counting measure a first-class citizen and identifies the unit-mass axiom as the ONLY difference between our two gauges. Its engineering lesson (defer normalization into the base measure; the paper measures 76% wasted runtime on normalization) is our integer-gauge rationale in different words: staying in the counting gauge is the cheap, integer thing.

## 5. One-liner

This paper gives us a clean, quotable identity — a measure and a probability differ only by the unit-mass axiom — which is exactly our [count]/[prob] register shift, plus an engineering proof (76% of runtime wasted on normalization) that staying in the counting gauge, as our fixed-point integer stack does, is the cheap register.
