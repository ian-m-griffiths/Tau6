# Introduction to Measure and Integration Theory
Sandoval-Romero, Reyna-Castañeda & Cedeño-Pérez (UNAM), arXiv:2606.05215v1 (2026). Undergraduate lecture notes (Part I: The Lebesgue Integral, ch. 1–8): σ-algebras → measures → Lebesgue integral → Lp spaces → modes of convergence. A *foundational* text: no group structure, no Haar/Radon/translation-invariance anywhere — the counting measure is its one "integer" object, and change-of-measure (§7.5) is its one "gauge" idea.

## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **C1 — "the integral is about partitioning the domain" is the wrong primitive.** Ch 1 builds Riemann on partitions of the interval; Prop 1.6 (Dirichlet) kills it and Thm 5.32 replaces it with "continuous λ a.e." — the correct primitive is the **measure of sets** (partition the range: `Σ αⱼ·µ(Aⱼ)`, Def 5.1), not the length of domain slices. (Reversal of the naive Riemann framing; the paper's own spine Ch 1 → Ch 5.)
- **C2 — "same total mass ⇒ same measure" is FALSE.** Ex 4.37: `µ(X)=ν(X)=1` and agreement on a non-π-system C, yet `µ≠ν`. Uniqueness (Thm 4.36) needs agreement on a **π-system** + equal total mass. Nuance for our gauge claim: count→probability rescale is information-preserving *not* because of the total mass alone but because it is a uniform rescale (a genuine density ϑ=1/T applied to every set).
- **C3 — "a measure must be finite/normalized to be useful" is FALSE.** The counting measure on N has `µ♯(N)=+∞` yet all theorems apply to it unchanged (MCT/Fatou Cor 5.21, ℓp = Lp Def 7.10, L9788: the only µ♯-null set is ∅). Normalization is optional and comes last. Direct support for "counts, not probabilities".
- **C4 — "a.e. convergence implies Lp convergence" is FALSE** (paper-internal). `fₖ := k·χ[0,1/k]` (type) converges λ a.e. to 0 but not in mean p (L11011–11012); Riesz–Weyl (Thm 8.15) gives only the correct one-way bridge.

## 5. One-liner

**This undergraduate text supplies the measure-theoretic foundation underneath our whole engine: the counting measure is the canonical integer-valued measure, sums and integrals are the same object under it, and the §7.5 change-of-measure theorem is the general form of our count↔probability gauge shift (a register shift by the total mass T) — direct, proved support for "counts, not probabilities" and for the gauge as a cheap integer thing; but it contains no translation invariance, so the Z₆/isotropy leg of the lens stays OURS.**
