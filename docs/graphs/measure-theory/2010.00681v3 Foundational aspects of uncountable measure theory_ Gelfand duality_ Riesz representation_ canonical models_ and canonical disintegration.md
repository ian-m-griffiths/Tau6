## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **The whole paper is a reversal of "measure theory needs countable/Polish hypotheses".** Its point: ALL the classical theorems (Riesz, Kolmogorov extension, disintegration) survive — or need genuine fixes — when you drop separability, and the fixes are category-theoretic (castings, canonical models). Counter-to the folklore that uncountable spaces are intractable.
- **Baire < Borel, and that's a feature**: individual points/compact sets can be non-measurable in the Baire algebra, yet the paper prefers Baire ("less pathological") — reversal of the usual reflex to want the biggest σ-algebra.
- **Probability algebras make problems disappear by deletion**: quotienting out null sets removes the "uncountable union of null sets is null" difficulty before it arises — the abstraction is the fix, not the loss.
- **τ-smoothness = "assigns no mass to βX\X"**: a functional that is NOT representable on X becomes representable on the compactification — the state/measure gap is a compactification phenomenon (Ex 5.6, Dieudonné Ex 5.7).

## 5. One-liner

This paper gives us the map of where measure theory is expensive — uncountable, regularity, measurability pathologies — and thereby the proof that the finite counting-measure case (our whole integer stack) is the trivial, cheap corner of the theory, with Riesz duality and casting functors as the paper's own gauge-shift machinery.
