# Data-inspired advances in geometric measure theory: generalized surface and shape metrics
Sharif N. Ibrahim, PhD dissertation, Washington State University (arXiv:1408.5954v1, 2014; advisor K. Vixie). Three advances: (1) the **multiscale simplicial flat norm** — a discrete, scale-λ version of the flat norm on currents, solvable as integer linear programming; (2) **integral flat-norm decompositions** — integral currents admit integer-valued optimal decompositions under a no-torsion condition; (3) **nonasymptotic densities** (integral area invariants) as shape signatures — fixed-radius un-normalized area data that reconstruct polygons and curves.

## 3. Counter-to / reversal edges

- **C1 — "Passing to the limit gives the truest signature" is FALSE.** The asymptotic density `Θ₂ = lim_{r↓0} Area/πr²` is `1/2` at *every* smooth boundary point — it cannot distinguish any two C¹ shapes. The **nonasymptotic** (fixed-radius, un-normalized) `g(s,r)` carries the shape information (§4.2, rem 4.2.2). The limit *destroys* information; the finite-scale count keeps it.
- **C2 — "Closest in mass = best approximation" is FALSE.** The inscribed n-gon is mass-distance 4π from the circle yet flat-norm-close — the correct notion of closeness allows a deformation `∂S` to cancel the difference (N12). Similarity is a *decomposition*, not a pointwise norm.
- **C3 — "Integer restrictions make problems harder" is FALSE here.** Integrality is a *feature*: under no-relative-torsion the optimal flat-norm decomposition is attained by integer chains, and the ILP is solvable in polynomial time (§2.3–2.4). The hard NP-hard case is the *general* (non-torsion-free) one.
- **C4 — "Geometric measure theory is computationally infeasible" is FALSE (partial).** The flat norm was long seen as purely theoretical; the simplicial deformation theorem + ILP formulation make it polynomial-time computable on torsion-free complexes (abstract, §2.1).
- **C5 — (scope) "Normalizing by πr² is harmless."** The paper explicitly leaves the normalization factor *out* because it is trivial to re-add when r is fixed — but the reconstruction theorems use the un-normalized g, i.e. the count form is the working object (rem 4.2.2).

## 5. One-liner

**The strongest single-paper support yet for Ian's "counts not probabilities / gauge as cheap integer" thesis: the asymptotic, normalized density provably destroys all information while the raw fixed-scale count reconstructs the object, and under a structural (no-torsion) condition the optimal decomposition is provably integer-valued — both moves are literally our count-gauge and integer-only register, appearing inside geometric measure theory.**
