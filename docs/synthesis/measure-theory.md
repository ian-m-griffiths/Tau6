## 2. The confirmed core — DIRECT mappings (paper + our Lean file)

1. **Counting measure is the base; Card(A) = Σ δ_ωj (Dirac density).** Lo (1711.04625 §04.14b) *defines* the counting measure as the sum of Dirac masses with ν(A) = Card(A) and calls it the conceptually central measure "all other measures constructed from it"; Doberkat (1409.2662 Prop 2.194) proves discrete combinations Σ r_k·δ_xk are **dense** in the space of measures; Sandoval (2606.05215 Ex 4.6) has µ♯(A) = |A| as "the canonical integer-valued measure"; Velhinho (2312.04365 L1195) shows the counting measure is the one translation-invariant measure that *survives* infinite dimensions. **Our side:** the stored primitive is the integer count — `Residual.lean` (INDEX R1) `E = f(a)f(b)/T`, `sum_E_row`, `sum_residual_eq_zero`; our whole stack lives in this measure (`LATTICE_MATH.md` integer register).
2. **Probability = renormalized counting.** Banica (2506.15534 Prop 14.12, Def 5.24): probability measure = the measure rescaled to µ(X)=1; finite-group Haar is µ(E)=|E|/|G| — literally counts ÷ total. Scherrer & Schauer (2110.00602 §2): a measure and a probability differ **only by the unit-mass axiom**. Papachristodoulos (1702.07154 §1): natural density d(A) = |{k∈A:k≤n}|/n is the counting measure read in the probability gauge. **Our side:** the `[count]→[prob]` register shift `p(a)p(b) = f(a)f(b)/T² = E/T` (GAUGE_VARIANTS §1B) — recorded as an arithmetic identity, no Lean file needed. Kreuzer's dyadic `λ([s]) = 2^−lth(s)` (1312.1531) is the same "renormalized counting" at its cheapest — powers of two, our shifts.
3. **Translation invariance / Haar = isotropy (Z₆).** Velhinho (2312.04365 §8): on a countable discrete group the counting measure **is** the Haar measure; on compact groups Haar = the normalized counting measure. Banica (2506.15534): Haar is unique translation-invariant averaging, independent of the seed (Prop 14.14 — his "gauge-invariant averaging" ≈ our δ invariant); the cyclotomic measure on 2n-th roots of unity has n=3 = the 6th roots = our Z₆ (Def 13.27). Lo (1711.04625 Ex 1(b)): the Lebesgue measure is *characterized* by translation invariance. FMT side (2101.08112, 1207.4950): translation invariance is the symmetry that diagonalizes/cheapens the correlation structure. **Our side:** `Gauge.lean` (INDEX G1) `norm_of_unit`, `norm_mul_unit`, `norm_eq_det`, `units_eq_omega_powers` — the norm is invariant under the six rotations. Calibration honesty: the *norm* invariance is PROVED in Lean; the "counting measure on ℤ[ω] = Haar under Z₆" is the **next theorem**, not yet proved (1711.04625 flags the same gap).
4. **Independence = product measure → r = O − E.** Lo (1808.01713 Def 3): independence of random variables is *defined* as the joint law being the **product measure of the marginals** — this is exactly our E; the signed residual `r = O − E` (INDEX R1) is by definition the deviation from the paper's Definition 3. Product-measure constructions appear identically in Doberkat (1409.2662 §2.9), Velhinho (2312.04365 Thm 4), Lo (1711.04625 (0.3)). **Our side:** `Residual.lean` R1 — `E` is the product of marginals ÷T; `sum_E_row` (Σ_b E(a,b) = f(a)); `sum_residual_eq_zero`; `ringSq_nonneg` (χ² ≥ 0).
5. **Measure-as-gauge is a conservative / eliminable add-on.** Kreuzer (1312.1531 Thm 3): adding the full Lebesgue measure to ACA0^ω+(µ) is **Π¹₂-conservative** — it proves nothing new; it is a definitional layer, and a term for λ(X) is computable (program extraction). This is the strongest possible formal statement of "the gauge is a cheap add-on." Scherrer & Schauer (2110.00602 §10) give the engineering twin: normalization is the enemy — 76% of LKJ runtime is spent on a normalization constant nobody uses; push it into the base and defer. Affeldt & Cohen (2209.02345 §7): Lean's mathlib already formalizes the outer-measure/Carathéodory route + Fubini + Radon–Nikodým + **Haar (van Doorn)**; on finite sets all the extension machinery collapses to triviality. **Our side:** the gauge doctrine (GAUGE_VARIANTS §5.4 "a 'failed' claim is usually a TRANSLATION/normalisation difference") now has a theorem behind it — from the literature, not from us.
6. **Density = ratio against a reference measure (Radon–Nikodym family).** Doberkat (1409.2662 §2.11.3), Lo (1808.01713 Ch 2.5), Velhinho (2312.04365 Thm 12), Banica (2506.15534 Thm 7.33): ν ≪ µ ⇔ ν(A) = ∫_A f dµ. **Our side:** `Registers.lean` R2 `δ_eq_residual_div` — δ = O/E − 1 is the discrete density of O wrt the null E; `surprise_eq_delta_sq_mul_E` is the χ² summand. ANALOGY (RN is a nonneg continuous density; our δ is a signed integer ratio −1), same shape.
7. **Un-normalized counts are the *information-bearing* object.** Ibrahim (1408.5954 §4.2) proves the asymptotic *normalized* density Θ₂ = 1/2 at every smooth boundary point — the limit destroys all shape information — while the raw fixed-scale count g(s,r) reconstructs the shape (Thm 4.6.1/4.7.1); and under a no-torsion condition the optimal flat-norm decomposition is **integer-valued** (§2.4). Lafuente & Cuesta (0205648 §2): on a lattice the additivity structure is integer-parity and the Mayer decomposition is *non-unique* — counting **creates** gauge freedom. Both are the thesis "gauge becomes a cheap integer thing" from inside the mathematics.

## 6. The counter-to / reversal edges the folder surfaced (all support the thesis)

The graphs surfaced seven genuine reversals of classical intuitions; each lands on the counts side:

1. **"A lattice theory is just the continuum one discretized" is FALSE** (0205648 C1/C2): on a lattice the Mayer decomposition is *non-unique* (a gauge freedom introduced by counting) and the additive/nonadditive split is integer-parity structure that disappears in the continuum limit. Counting does not approximate the continuum — it adds structure.
2. **Egorov's theorem fails in the counting/statistical gauge** (1702.07154 C1): the "measure-side" theorems (Riesz, dominated convergence) survive statistically, the "pointwise-side" ones (Egorov) do not. Classical theorems transfer gauge-by-gauge, not automatically — the project's expired-claims discipline, now with a citation.
3. **The continuum is the harder gauge, not the more fundamental one** (1409.2662 C1): in large/continuous spaces point-to-point probabilities cease to exist and must be replaced by transition *measures to sets*; the discrete case is the simple one.
4. **Sum-to-1 is optional** (0906.0294 C1, N16): the quantum measure refuses normalization (unitality is an extra axiom, values exceed 1) — probability is not forced by measurehood.
5. **The measure is cheap, not heavy** (1312.1531 C1/C2): full Lebesgue measure on all subsets of 2^N is consistent in a weak arithmetic without an inaccessible cardinal, and is Π¹₂-conservative — it adds no content. Measure-as-gauge, not measure-as-content.
6. **The normalized limit destroys information** (1408.5954 C1): Θ₂ = 1/2 at every smooth boundary point; only the un-normalized fixed-scale count reconstructs the shape.
7. **Precision and consistency are competing gauges** (1207.4950 C2; 2009.09390 C1): CS is more precise than PY but breaks thermodynamic consistency; explicit stability forces giving up low-density accuracy — you pick the gauge, you don't get both. Same trade-off shape as our "retire classical theorems to keep an integer gauge" discipline.

## 7. Guardrails — what we must NOT claim from this folder

1. **Not "our lattice is quantum measure theory."** 9401003's own graph is explicit: our counting measure sits at Sorkin's classical level (n=2); his quantum measure is a third object (real-valued, bi-additive, frequency-uninterpretable). Claiming the identification would re-litigate the retired quantum-superposition claim. The paper's *framing* supports us; its *objects* don't.
2. **Not "the hex-lattice Haar theorem is proved."** `proofs/INDEX.md` has G1 (norm isotropy: `norm_of_unit`, `norm_mul_unit`, `norm_eq_det`, `units_eq_omega_powers`) — the *norm* side. The "counting measure on ℤ[ω] = Haar under Z₆" theorem is §5 proposal #1, not a result (1711.04625's graph flags the same gap).
3. **Come-to-terms before citing FMT papers as measure theory.** In 0205648 / 2101.08112 / 1207.4950 / 2009.09390, "measure" = geometric valuation/weighted density (Hadwiger intrinsic volumes), not a σ-additive measure. Their evidence for "counts not probabilities" is real but analogical; do not cite them for measure-theoretic statements.
4. **The ring is a χ² divergence value, not Fisher information** (retired claim; GAUGE_VARIANTS §3C guardrail). Nothing in this folder resurrects it.
5. **"Lattice", "orthogonal", "T" term collisions stand** (2506.15534 C2/C3; 9401003's T): Banica's "lattice" is the partition poset; RN's "orthogonal" is mutual singularity (not our ⊥ axis); Sorkin's T is truncation time (not our corpus total). Do not map on shared words.
