# Balanced-Ternary Logic Gates — Survey & Calibrated Verdict

**2026-08-29 — component survey: the logic-gate layer (Wave 1, item 1 of
`docs/TERNARY_COMPUTE_SURVEY.md`).** Companion files, same wave:

- `gate_area.md` — the **measured** gate-area benchmark (yosys + SkyWater 130nm). This
  file cites its numbers rather than re-deriving them.
- `arithmetic.md` — adder/multiplier/divide/compare/rotate (arithmetic units).
- `control.md` — ternary opcodes vs binary-host control.

**Calibration legend (house standard, `docs/MAP_BRIEF.md`):**

- **DIRECT** — measured or proved (our ngspice/yosys numbers, our Lean proofs, or a citable
  literature number).
- **ANALOGY** — parallel structure to something real, but the mapping is not an identity
  (e.g. CNTFET transistor counts ported to our CMOS flow; unbalanced {0,1,2} results).
- **OURS** — our own design claim, carried from project files (RTL, Lean ledger).
- **SPECULATION** — untested hypothesis, flagged as such.

**One-line answer up front:** balanced ternary gives **one genuinely free gate** (negation —
a wire swap, 0.00 µm² measured) and **one complete primitive set that mirrors binary's field
algebra** (`{mod-3 sum, mod-3 product}` = `{tadd1.sum, tmul}` — the finite-field F₃ analogue of
binary `{XOR, AND}`); everything else is more expensive than binary, and the **2-threshold
receiver tax is real and measured** (ternary MIN/MAX = 2.00× a binary AND/OR, mod-3 sum =
4.33× a binary full adder). Ternary gates do **not** beat binary on gate-level area or
energy; the measured ternary wins are at the *wire* (the free null), not the *gate*.

## 1. Encoding & the frame — what a "ternary gate" is here

Trit = 2 wires, one-hot-per-direction (`rtl/trit_functions.vh`, Lean-proved in
`TernaryCell.lean`):

```
value  bits   energized wires
 +1    2'b01  push only
  0    2'b00  neither (null — nothing energized)
 -1    2'b10  pull only
 11    2'b11  BOTH — NEVER produced (free don't-care input)
```

Consequences that govern everything below:

- **Negation is free** — `tneg = {t[0], t[1]}` is a wire permutation, no logic. **[OURS,
  measured 0.00 µm² / 0 cells — `gate_area.md`]**
- **Every other gate is "two binary thresholds."** Because a trit lives on 2 wires, a ternary
  gate is synthesized from binary standard cells; a MIN gate is literally one AND2 (the `+`
  line) plus one OR2 (the `−` line). **[DIRECT — `gate_area.md`]**
- **The null is free on the wire, not in the gate.** On a wire, "send 0" costs ~0.05 pJ
  (24× cheaper than a ±1). But a *gate* must still resolve "is this −1, 0, or +1?" every
  cycle — it cannot skip work on a null input. This is the single most important distinction
  for the verdict. **[OURS — follows from Law 1 + the reframe in `TERNARY_COMPUTE_SURVEY.md`]**

### The 2-threshold vs 1-threshold receiver tax (the honest frame)

**[DIRECT — `docs/ENERGY_LAWS.md` Law 1 + measured in `circuit/ENERGY_RESULTS.md`]**

- A **binary** gate resolves 1 state boundary → **1 threshold** (one comparator).
- A **level-coded ternary** gate resolves 3 voltage levels → **2 thresholds** (two comparators).
- Our **polarity** receiver demuxes push/pull onto 2 rails, each with its own sense amp → also
  **2 detection paths** per trit, but the null is "neither rail crosses" (no third comparator).

The tax is *measured*, not hypothetical: the receiver's share of symbol energy went
**13% → 61% → 67%** as the wire swing dropped (Law 1), because the wire cost shrinks ∝V²
while the measurement cost is fixed. PAM-4's 3-comparator receiver costs **0.128 pJ/bit**
vs ternary's 2-sense-amp **0.032–0.05 pJ/trit** and binary's 1 comparator. More states =
more thresholds = more fixed receiver energy. A ternary gate pays 2 thresholds to a binary
gate's 1, **forever** — this is the wall the whole survey tests.

The one offset: a trit carries **log₂3 ≈ 1.585 bits** [DIRECT — `RadixEconomy.lean`], so per
*bit of information* the receiver penalty is **2 / 1.585 ≈ 1.26×**, not 2×. Whether that
0.26× residue is recovered depends on whether the *encoding* (2 wires/trit) and the *gate
fan-in* give the density back. The area data (§5) says they do not, on a binary standard-cell
library.

## 2. Unary gates

Balanced ternary has 3³ = 27 unary functions. The useful ones are the **symmetry group of the
3 digits** (6 permutations) plus the **3 constants**. The group is **D₃ ≅ S₃**, generated by
one reflection + one 3-cycle.

| gate | function on {−1,0,+1} | our cell | cost | calibration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **identity** | x → x | (wire) | 0 | DIRECT |
| **negation** (`tneg`/STI) | +1↔−1, 0→0 (a *reflection*) | `tneg` = wire swap | **0.00 µm², 0 cells** | OUR cells: DIRECT measured; literature STI: 4–6 CNFET (level-coded) |
| **cycle +1** (successor, ×ω) | −1→0→+1→−1 | `tadd1(x,+1,0).sum` | = tadd1, no new cell | DIRECT (the 3-cycle of 2305.04115 ROTATE, unbalanced) |
| **cycle −1** (predecessor, ×ω²) | +1→0→−1→+1 | `tadd1(x,−1,0).sum` | = tadd1, no new cell | DIRECT |
| identity-as-3rd-cycle | x → x | — | 0 | DIRECT (the trivial element of Z₃) |

Notes:

- **Negation is the reflection, the cycles are the rotations.** Our Z₆ (the six 60° units)
  contains their Z₃ as `⟨ω²⟩` [DIRECT — `Rotation.lean`, `docs/synthesis/ternary-circuits.md`].
- **The cycle is the completeness-critical operator.** min/max/negation alone cannot *generate*
  a cycle (they are all monotone in the trit order — see §4). We get the cycle for free as a
  special case of `tadd1` with a constant operand, **but we do not expose it as a named cell**;
  the literature names it explicitly (ROTATE, `2305.04115`). **[OURS — implementation gap, not
  a math gap]**
- The **3 constants** {−1, 0, +1} are free: `2'b10`, `2'b00`, `2'b01` are hardwired data
  values, no gates. This matters for completeness (§4) — field completeness needs constants,
  and ours cost nothing.

## 3. Binary gates

| gate | definition on {−1,0,+1} (order −1<0<+1) | our cell | measured area | literature (CNFET) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **min / AND** (`tand`) | lesser trit (lattice *meet*) | `tand` = `{a1\|b1, a0&b0}` | 12.512 µm², 2 cells | TMIN/TAND = min [DIRECT, `2309.01615`, `2211.04542`, `1807.06419`] |
| **max / OR** (`tor`) | greater trit (lattice *join*) | `tor` = `{a1&b1, a0\|b0}` | 12.512 µm², 2 cells | TMAX/TOR = max [DIRECT] |
| **NAND** (`tnand`) | ¬(min) | `tneg(tand(x,y))` | +0 (wire swap on min) | TNAND [DIRECT, `2211.04542`] |
| **NOR** (`tnor`) | ¬(max) | `tneg(tor(x,y))` | +0 (wire swap on max) | TNOR [DIRECT, `2305.04115`] |
| **mod-3 sum** (ternary XOR) | a+b mod 3, carry *dropped* | `tadd1(x,y,0).sum` | = tadd1 | XOR = mod-3 sum [DIRECT, `2211.12176` Table 1] |
| **product** (`tmul`) | sign product, 0 absorbs | `tmul` | *not in gate_area benchmark* (≈4 AND2 + 2 OR2 ≈ 6 cells) | MUL = sign product [DIRECT, `2309.01615` Table 7] |
| **full adder** (`tadd1`) | `{cout, sum}` balanced carry | `tadd1` | **146.39 µm², 25 cells** | 3-operand balanced FA 118–188 tr [DIRECT, `Automated_synthesis`] |
| **majority / median** (3-in) | middle value of 3 trits | (not a cell) | — | ≈ the 3-carry cell, **50 tr** [DIRECT, `Automated_synthesis`] |
| **consensus** (2-in) | x if x==y else 0 ("agreement") | `tmul`-adjacent | — | standard MVL op; *no circuit corpus entry* |
| **implication** | ¬x ∨ y (Kleene K3) | `tor(tneg(x), y)` = max(−x, y) | +0 (free neg + 1 max) | standard L3/K3; *no circuit corpus entry* |

Truth-table ground truth (balanced, order −1<0<+1) — the min/max/neg tables are **bit-for-bit
identical** across `2309.01615`, `2211.04542`, `1807.06419` and our `trit_functions.vh`
[DIRECT — `docs/synthesis/ternary-circuits.md` §2a]:

```
tneg:  -1→+1, 0→0, +1→-1          (reflection)
cycle: -1→0,  0→+1, +1→-1         (successor = +1 mod 3)
tand (min):  min over -1<0<+1      tor (max):  max over -1<0<+1
tmul:  a·b = sign(a)·sign(b), 0 absorbs
tadd1.sum:  a+b+cin mod 3 ; carry: +1 iff s≥2, -1 iff s≤-2
```

Two calibration flags on the under-sourced gates:

- **"consensus" collides with binary.** In binary, consensus = majority = `xy∨xz∨yz`. In the
  ternary MVL minimization literature, "consensus" is usually the 2-input *agreement*
  operation (`x` if `x==y` else `0`), used by the ternary consensus theorem. They are different
  functions; the corpus I have does not contain a fabricated "consensus" circuit, so its cost
  above is **ANALOGY (standard MVL), not measured**. **[come-to-terms flag]**
- **"majority" and "median" coincide for 3 trits** (the median is the value occurring ≥2
  times). Its closest fabricated realization is the 3-operand *carry* cell (50 tr,
  `Automated_synthesis`), which is a *threshold* on the digit sum, not the value-median — close,
  not identical. **[ANALOGY]**

## 4. Functional completeness — the gate-set table

This is where ternary genuinely differs from binary, and it is the intellectual core of the
survey.

| gate set | complete? | why / source |
|---|---|---|
| `{NAND}` (binary) | ✅ | binary is the one radix with a Sheffer stroke |
| `{NOR}` (binary) | ✅ | ditto |
| **any single 2-input ternary function** | ❌ | **no ternary NAND/NOR exists** — for radix ≥3 there is no binary Sheffer function [DIRECT — Martin 1954, *The Sheffer functions of 3-valued logic*] |
| `{min, max, negation}` | ❌ | min/max are monotone and negation is order-*reversing*; the closure of `{min,max,neg}` (even with constants) is the **lattice-polynomial fragment** — it can never produce the cyclic or mod-3 structure [DIRECT] |
| `{min, max, all 27 unary}` | ✅ | Słupecki criterion: all unary + an *essential* function (min is surjective) [DIRECT — Słupecki 1939] |
| `{ROTATE, ALPHA(min), BETA(max), GAMMA}` | ✅ | "one monadic + three dyadic operators express any ternary calculation" [DIRECT — `2305.04115`, proved] |
| **`{mod-3 sum, mod-3 product, constants}`** | ✅ | **Lagrange interpolation over the finite field F₃** — every function F₃ⁿ→F₃ is a polynomial in + and × [DIRECT — standard finite-field algebra] |
| **`{tneg, tand, tor, tmul, tadd1}` (our cells)** | ✅ | follows from the F₃ row: `tmul` = F₃×, `tadd1.sum` = F₃+, constants are free hardwired trits; the min/max/neg trio is redundant for completeness [OURS — a direct corollary, **not separately proved in the ledger**] |

**The one-line synthesis:** binary is complete with *one* gate (NAND). Ternary is complete
with *two* gates — **mod-3 sum + mod-3 product** (plus free constants) — which is exactly the
finite-field algebra of F₃, the ternary twin of binary's `{XOR, AND}` over F₂. min/max/negation,
which *look* like the natural ternary `{AND, OR, NOT}`, are **not** complete: they are the
*lattice* fragment, and lattice operations cannot produce the *cyclic* (rotation) or *field*
(mod-3) structure. The cycle (`ROTATE`, our `tadd1(x,+1,0).sum`) is the missing third operator
the literature explicitly adds — and it is precisely the operator our RTL only has *implicitly*,
as a special case of the adder.

### 5c. The honest read

- **The 2-threshold tax is measured exactly:** MIN = AND2 + OR2 = 2.00× a binary AND/OR. A
  ternary MIN is two binary thresholds.
- **The mod-3 sum is the expensive one (4.33× a binary FA).** A balanced full adder must
  threshold for both carry directions *and* both sum directions simultaneously.
- **Per bit the numbers are worse still.** A trit = 1.585 bits on **2 wires** = 0.792 bits/wire
  vs binary's 1 bit/wire — ternary wastes 26% of its wires *and* pays 2.00–4.33× per gate.
  There is no gate class where 1.585 bits/trit compensates. **[DIRECT — `gate_area.md`]**

## 6. Energy vs binary

The energy story splits cleanly into **wire** (communication) and **gate** (compute), and the
split is the whole point.

### 6a. The wire (communication) — where ternary actually wins

**[DIRECT — measured, ngspice 44.2 fair-fight, `circuit/ENERGY_RESULTS.md`]**

| scheme | pJ/bit | vs binary (0.748) |
|---|---|---|
| binary | 0.748 | 1.00× |
| **ternary (null-carrying, uniform)** | **0.515** | 1.45× better |
| PAM-4 | 0.401 | 1.86× |
| PWM-5 2D | 0.397 | 1.88× |
| low-swing × resonant | **0.081** | 9.2× |

> **⚠️ Baseline correction (`FINAL_VERDICT.md`):** the "vs binary (0.748)" column above uses
> the *retired* 0.748 pJ/bit reference. The fair-fight correction makes the champion
> 0.081 pJ/bit **6.3× vs natural single-ended binary (0.512)** and **2.67× vs matched
> low-swing binary (0.216)** — the honest settled range is ~2.7–6.3×, not 9.2×.

Ternary's wire win is *entirely* the free null: a ±1 costs ~1.2 pJ to drive but the null costs
~0.05 pJ, and the null is a **data-bearing symbol** (0 is a real balanced-ternary value). The
average (1.20+1.20+0.05)/3 = 0.817 pJ/trit = **0.515 pJ/bit**. **This is a transport win, not a
gate win.**

### 6b. The gate (compute) — the tax applies here

- **A gate cannot skip work on a null input.** It must still resolve −1/0/+1 every cycle. The
  free-null argument does not transfer from the wire to the gate. **[OURS — the `TERNARY_COMPUTE_SURVEY.md`
  reframe]**
- The literature's *own* honest results are mixed, and every win is conditional:
  - `2211.12176` (multi-Vt CMOS TLG): STI / comparator / XOR / half-adder **beat** CMOS-only,
    but **AND/OR lose** — because the clocked threshold gate carries a flip-flop and the
    min/max gates needed extra ternary D-latches for synchronization. [DIRECT]
  - `2211.04542` (CNFET): 88–99% PDP cuts — but only with CNTFET devices **and** an external
    mid-rail (the "dual-supply" trick; the naive divider-made middle level is a static
    Vdd→GND path and an energy *liability*). [DIRECT]
  - `Automated_synthesis` (CNTFET): the 3-operand FA wins PDP, but only as a *holistic*
    non-compound cell, not 2-operand composition. [DIRECT]
- **We have not yet measured gate energy.** The yosys power pass is still a backlog item
  (`STATE_NOTE.md`; the current OpenSTA build reports switching 1.60 mW for the whole CPU but
  not internal/dynamic power per gate). So there is **no DIRECT per-gate joule number for our
  ternary cells** — only the area numbers of §5 and the literature's PDP numbers above.

## 7. The honest verdict — can ternary gates beat binary?

**No — not on a binary standard-cell library, and the 2-threshold tax is the reason.**

1. **The tax is real and measured, not hypothetical.** MIN/MAX = 2.00× a binary AND/OR; the
   mod-3 sum = 4.33× a binary full adder; the encoding wastes 26% of wires (0.792 bits/wire).
   [DIRECT — `gate_area.md`]

2. **The free null does not rescue the gate.** The null is free *on the wire* (nothing to
   transmit). A gate must still *resolve* −1/0/+1 every toggle, so the null's freedom is a
   communication property, not a computation property. The measured ternary wins (0.515 pJ/bit,
   0.081 pJ/bit) are all at the transport layer. [OURS + DIRECT]

3. **The literature agrees, with the honest caveats visible.** Ternary gates only win when (a)
   the *device* is native multi-valued (CNTFET threshold-via-chirality, memristor, multi-Vt
   CMOS), (b) the middle state is made *without* a divider (external rail, or our no-drive
   null), and (c) cells are built as holistic 3-operand relations, not 2-input chains. On plain
   CMOS with an encoding layered on binary standard cells, ternary gates lose. [DIRECT]

4. **What ternary *does* buy at the gate level** is real but narrow: a free inverter (0.00 µm²),
   a structurally cheaper negate/rotate/round-to-nearest (see `arithmetic.md`), and one fewer
   symbol per unit information (log₂3) — but none of these offsets a 2.00–4.33× per-gate area
   multiplier.

**The threshold where the verdict flips:** ternary gates become competitive **only** if the
*trit* is a native 3-state device (one wire, three physically distinct states, one
thresholding device per state) — at which point the 2-wire/2-threshold overhead of our
one-hot encoding collapses. That is a *transistor-technology* bet (CNTFET, memristor,
reconfigurable-polarity FET — the `Yeom 2025` / `2309.01615` direction), **not** an RTL bet.
Until then, the honest architecture is the one `cpu.v` already uses: **ternary datapath for
the arithmetic symmetry + binary control and binary standard cells underneath**, paying the
2-threshold tax only where the ternary symmetry (negation, Z₆ rotation, balanced carry) earns
it back. [OURS + DIRECT]

## 8. The most economical primitive

Ranked, calibrated:

1. **Cheapest gate: negation (`tneg`) — 0.00 µm², 0 cells, Lean-proved.** A wire swap. It is
   the one place balanced ternary beats binary unconditionally. **[DIRECT]**

2. **Cheapest complete set: `{mod-3 sum, mod-3 product}` + free constants = `{tadd1.sum, tmul}`.**
   This is the F₃ field pair, the exact ternary twin of binary's `{XOR, AND}` (F₂). It is
   *minimal* in the field sense (two binary operations + constants, same as binary), and it is
   the set whose completeness is a one-line corollary (Lagrange interpolation over F₃), not a
   hand-waved analogy. **[OURS — corollary of DIRECT finite-field algebra]**

3. **Cheapest building material: `{min, max, negation}` at 12.5 µm² each (negation free).** The
   lattice trio is the cheapest per-gate primitive **but is incomplete** — it cannot make a
   cycle or a mod-3 sum. Use it for the *logic* slice of a datapath, never as the sole library.

4. **The practical primitive for a real processor: `tadd1` + `tmul`.** `tadd1` is the expensive
   cell (146 µm²) but it is load-bearing three times over: its **sum** is the mod-3 sum (the
   completeness ingredient), its **carry** is the balanced positional carry (the arithmetic
   ingredient), and with a constant operand it **generates the cycle** (the third completeness
   ingredient). `tmul` supplies the field product. So the *minimal complete gate library for
   balanced ternary is exactly two cells* — `{tadd1, tmul}` — and everything else (neg, min,
   max, the cycle) is either free or derivable. This mirrors binary, where the minimal complete
   practical core is `{XOR, AND}` and NOT/NAND/NOR are conveniences.

**Bottom line:** the economical primitive is **not** the naive `{AND, OR, NOT}`-analog
(min/max/neg — cheap but incomplete), and **not** a single gate (none exists). It is the
**finite-field pair `{mod-3 sum, mod-3 product}`**, realized in our cells as `{tadd1, tmul}`,
with negation and constants free. That is the balanced-ternary Sheffer *set* — the smallest
family that is both complete and matched to the F₃ arithmetic the processor already runs on.
